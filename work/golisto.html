02 · Situation

Golisto is a dedicated marketplace for retro gaming and collectibles, trading across the EU. It spans video games and consoles, trading cards, figures, sports cards, comics, films and memorabilia — seven categories that share a buyer but almost nothing else.

In collecting, condition is the price. A sealed copy and a loose cartridge of the same game are different objects, and every category says so in its own language: PSA grades for cards, complete-in-box for games, prose for memorabilia. On a general marketplace that difference gets buried in a description and a photo, which is where disputes come from. Golisto exists to put it in the structure instead.

03 · Problem

Buying a collectible online means taking a stranger’s word that the thing is real, and there is nothing standing behind that word.

Constraints
01

Every category grades differently.

A card is PSA 3. A console is complete in box. A game is sealed, or it is missing the battery cover. One listing model has to carry all of those without flattening them into a single star rating.

02

Strangers, borders, and no way to inspect.

Buyers and sellers are spread across the EU, in different postal systems, trading items they cannot hold first. Buyer protection, verified sellers and integrated payments are the reason a transaction happens at all.

03

Two selling models, one seller.

Fixed-price listings and live timed auctions run side by side, and the same person has to be able to list into either in under a minute.

04 · Decisions Template · 3 slots
D.01

Condition became a field instead of a sentence — in the app first.

On the table · Draft — confirm or correct

Three shapes were rejected. A free-text condition line — what the web listing still has, and what sellers default to everywhere else: “good/great, complete in box” typed under the description, unsearchable and impossible to dispute. One grade scale for everything, a star or a number, which puts a sealed cartridge and a loose disc on the same axis. And photos as the only evidence, which hands the whole judgement to the buyer. Third-party grades like PSA were kept as a field on the listing rather than made the field: most items on the marketplace are not graded and never will be. [Confirm: were these the actual alternatives, and was anything else on the table?]

Why this one

Condition is what sets the price, so it has to be searchable and comparable. Structured on the listing, it can be filtered, priced against, and held up when a buyer disputes a sale. Written into the description it is only a claim, and the dispute comes down to one person's word against a photograph.

What it cost · Draft — confirm or correct

Extra steps in the listing flow, per category, against a product that promises listing in under a minute. The tension was resolved by making condition a pick rather than a form: the two axes — completeness and wear — as single-tap choices, with the description kept for everything the fields cannot say. Web went second because the app is where a seller lists with the item in hand; the web listing still shows the coarse New / Used pair until the field ships there. [Confirm: the actual mechanics — defaults, optional fields, tap count — and whether web-second was sequencing or resourcing.]

Where it stands

The structured field ships in the iOS and Android apps. On the web listing it is still the coarse New / Used pair, with the real grade typed into the description — “Good/great, complete in box” on a PS2 listing, “PSA 3” on a graded card. The vocabulary itself is published in the Knowledge Hub: six grades on two axes, completeness crossed with wear.

D.02

Land one niche properly before opening the next.

On the table

A general collectibles marketplace from day one, against a sequence: toys first, then video games and consoles, then trading cards. The pre-seed deck committed to the sequence.

Why this one

Each niche grades its own way and has its own communities to recruit from. Opening all of them at once means a listing model that fits none of them properly and a cold start in every category simultaneously.

What it cost · Draft — confirm or correct

Sellers with the right inventory in the wrong year. The sequence meant asking card and LEGO sellers to wait while toys and games were built out, and a marketplace that looked smaller than its ambition to anyone counting categories. It also meant building the listing model more than once, per niche, instead of once badly for all of them. [Confirm: were sellers actually turned away, and was there pressure — from users or investors — to open categories early?]

Where it stands

Eight categories are live today: video games and consoles, collectible card games, figures and statues, collectible sports cards, LEGO, comics, movies, and entertainment memorabilia.

D.03

Protection sits inside the transaction, not beside it.

On the table · Draft — confirm or correct

Reputation only — ratings and reviews, the way the Facebook groups in the pitch deck policed themselves, after the fact. Manual review of every listing before it went live. Authentication as a gate, where nothing sells until a third party has verified it. Each was rejected: reputation arrives too late for a first trade between strangers, manual review does not scale past a few hundred listings a day, and a hard authentication gate would have removed the ordinary used game from the marketplace entirely. [Confirm.]

Why this one · Draft — confirm or correct

Trade protection on every in-app purchase, with the buyer’s money held until the item is confirmed; seller verification as a badge rather than a wall; ratings on top. Escrow is the one mechanism that works on the very first trade, when there is no history to lean on. Verification and ratings then compound on it rather than stand in for it. [Confirm: was escrow there from launch or added, and is “verified” a check or a paid tier?]

What it cost · Draft — confirm or correct

Margin and friction. Holding the money means Golisto carries the payment flow, the shipping integration and the disputes — the fee schedule and the trade-protection rules on the site are the visible price of that. And every step between “buy” and “paid” is a step where a buyer can leave. [Confirm: dispute volume, the fee level you are willing to state, and what protection excludes.]
