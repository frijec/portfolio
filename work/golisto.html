02 · Situation

Golisto is a dedicated marketplace for retro gaming and collectibles, trading across the EU. It spans video games and consoles, trading cards, figures, sports cards, comics, films and memorabilia — seven categories that share a buyer but almost nothing else.

In collecting, condition is the price. A sealed copy and a loose cartridge of the same game are different objects, and every category says so in its own language: PSA grades for cards, complete-in-box for games, prose for memorabilia. On a general marketplace that difference gets buried in a description and a photo, which is where disputes come from. Golisto exists to put it in the structure instead.

Constraints — template slots
01

Every category grades differently.

A card is PSA 3. A console is complete in box. A game is sealed, or it is missing the battery cover. One listing model has to carry all of those without flattening them into a single star rating.

02

Strangers, borders, and no way to inspect.

Buyers and sellers are spread across the EU, in different postal systems, trading items they cannot hold first. Buyer protection, verified sellers and integrated payments are the reason a transaction happens at all.

03

Two selling models, one seller.

Fixed-price listings and live timed auctions run side by side, and the same person has to be able to list into either in under a minute.

D.01

Condition is structured, so it can be filtered and disputed.

On the table

[What was rejected here — a free-text condition note, a single star rating, photos alone? Worth naming it.]

Why this one

Condition is what sets the price, so it has to be searchable and comparable. Structured on the listing, it can be filtered, priced against, and held up when a buyer disputes a sale. Written into the description it is only a claim, and the dispute comes down to one person's word against a photograph.
